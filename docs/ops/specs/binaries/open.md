# Technical Specification: open

Binary: `Sessions/ops/bin/open` (Python 3, standard library only; `mind.py` for the caller, and `packet.py` for the open packet and for the names of the delivery functions, each loaded by path; the packet tool's status and the gauge are asked as programs). Tests: `tools/test/test_open.py` (`TEST-56`). Spec writer: the operator's outside writer (an outside Claude, not a resident), 2026-11-12, after STELA's `ops/bin/open` and its spec (`Projects/STELA`). Status: built 2026-11-12 on the operator's direction; it shows and records, and no other tool requires it yet; wired to no hook and no timer.

## First Principles Rationale

The policy opens by saying that the system prevents and does not request. The read-in was the largest thing this room still requested. PoT 1.3.18 asks that required contracts are read before work that depends on them, and the read-in was a list of ten files and a manifest that said of itself that no program reads it. A resident could say it had read the law without the law ever having been on its screen, and nothing in the room could tell. PoT 2.1.1 calls a safeguard held only in memory a defect.

STELA met the same problem for its single-use threads with `ops/bin/open`: a freshness checkpoint with a trace, which its `certify` then demands. A thread that skips it knows nothing. A resident is not single-use, and its failure is the worse one: it is sure of a law it read three hundred turns ago, or before an amendment.

So an OPEN here differs from STELA's in four ways, each because of what a resident is.

- **It shows the law and does not point at it.** STELA points, because a bundle carries the files to a thread that cannot see the repository. Here everyone is local, so nothing is bundled or dumped, and the text itself is printed.
- **It finds the caller.** A typed name is an assumed identity. The tool resolves the registry key as the mind tool does.
- **It repeats what matters at every shift,** short and recent, and prints a whole file only when this body has not seen it as it now stands.
- **It writes down that it did:** who, when, which law by hash, which clauses, and what the caller said the shift is for.

One thing it keeps from STELA unchanged. STELA's `open` names the functions of the work and assigns them to nobody by identity; the active work contract assigns them. Here the active work contract is the open packet's leaf, which names by registry key who carries each of the four delivery functions (PoT 6.1.4, CONSTRAINTS C17). So the clauses a resident is shown for its work are the clauses of the functions that leaf gives it. A function is work in one packet (PoT 4.1.11). The tool reads it from the leaf and from nowhere else: nothing in the caller's registry entry and nothing a caller says decides which law it is shown.

## Mechanics and Sequencing

1. **The room and the law.** The room is the folder that holds this `ops/bin`. It must be the root of a Git checkout and not a linked worktree. The law in force is what the room's own `main` holds: `PoT.md`, `ops/lib/manifests/CONTEXT.md` and `ops/lib/manifests/CONSTRAINTS.md` are read from the Git objects of `main`. An uncommitted edit in the checkout is never shown. Nothing is fetched.
2. **The caller.** Resolved exactly as `mind.py` resolves it, through the room's canonical registry; another registry serves tests only, through that tool's seam. The entry must be registered once, to this room, and must name a body. From that entry the tool takes the key, the person's name for the report, and a digest of the session identifier. It never prints or stores the identifier.
3. **The table.** `CONTEXT.md` section X04 lists, by clause number, what is shown. Its first row is for every reader. Its other rows are the four delivery functions, by the names `packet.py` gives them, each once and no other: a table with any other row, or without one of the four, opens nothing for anyone. The text of a clause is read from `main` at the time of the OPEN. A row that cites a clause the policy does not hold, a retired clause or a missing section of the constraints opens nothing for anyone; the suite reads the room's own table and fails before such a change lands.
4. **The caller's functions.** The tool asks the packet tool's own reader for the open packet and takes the head of its leaf. The functions whose key is the caller's are the caller's functions in that packet. When no packet is open, when the open packet's leaf gives the caller none, or when the open packet cannot be read, the caller has none, and the rows of all four functions are shown, with the reason. A leaf that cannot be read gives no function: what the packet tool's ledger remembers of it is not taken in its place.
5. **What is printed,** in this order:
   - `[FRESHNESS]`: the caller, its functions in the open packet, the intent, the commit of `main`, the first twelve characters of each law file's SHA-256, the trace, and what the OPEN is good for.
   - `[THE LAW, WHOLE: <file>]`: each of the three files this body has not been shown as it now stands, entire.
   - `[EVERY READER]`: the clauses of the first row, in full. Each clause stands under the heading of the law it belongs to, with the words the law sets between that heading and its first clause, so that a clause such as a stop reads as what it is. A cited section of the constraints is printed whole under its name.
   - `[YOUR FUNCTION IN <packet>: <function>]`, once for each function the caller carries; or `[THE FUNCTIONS: all 4 are shown, because ...]` with the four rows when it carries none. A clause already shown is not printed twice.
   - `[PRESENT STATE]`: what `packet.py status` prints now, the newest entry of the ledger, what landed on `main` since the caller's last OPEN in this body, and the caller's fullness by the gauge's own reading, taken without a ledger line. Unknown is said.
   - `[THE BOARD]`: the sections `Now` and `Open` of `NOW.md` on `main`, as they stand.
   - `[REPORT]`: five lines for the caller to give the operator as they stand: identity by person and registry key, the function, parent authority, the OPEN with its trace, and fullness.
6. **Parts.** A part holds at most 9,000 bytes and 200 lines, so that no provider's shell tool cuts it. There are three runs of lines: the freshness lines with the law printed whole, the clauses, and the present state with the board and the report. A run joins the part before it when it fits there whole and begins a new part when it does not; a run larger than a part is cut where a part fills, never inside a line. With the law as it stands today a first OPEN is seven parts. An ordinary OPEN is one part when the caller carries one function, and two parts, the law and then the present state, when it carries more or when all four rows are shown. Each part begins and ends with its own line; `--next` prints the next. A new OPEN replaces an unfinished one and says so. An unfinished OPEN is dropped when its body changed, when the law changed while it was read, or when it waited more than an hour.
7. **The record.** When the last part has been printed, and not before, one line is appended to `logs/open/ledger.jsonl`: the trace, the times, the key, the digest of the body, the three hashes, the commit of `main`, the intent, the open packet, the caller's functions in it, the function rows shown, the files printed whole, the clauses cited, the number of parts, and the SHA-256 of everything printed. Files are mode 0600 in folders of mode 0700. Nothing is committed.
8. **Standing.** An OPEN is good for one body, one text of the three files and twelve hours, and only while it has shown the row of every function the open packet gives the caller now. An OPEN that showed all four rows therefore stays good when a packet names its holder; an OPEN that showed one function ends when a later packet gives its holder another, and the next OPEN shows that function's clauses. `--check` says whether the caller holds a good OPEN, and why not. The function `standing` answers the same question for a tool that would require an OPEN, for a named function.
9. **`--roster`.** One line for each resident registered to this room: a good OPEN with its intent, or none, with the reason. Any reader may run it; it is the operator's view of who is on shift under which law.
10. **An unverified reader** is shown the first row's clauses and the names of the three files, and is told that nothing was recorded. It is shown no function. It exits 2.
11. **Exit codes.** `0`: a part was printed, or the check passed. `2`: refused, unverified, or no good OPEN.

## Anecdotal Anchor

On 2026-11-12 this room took a new Git route, built by an outside writer who had been pointed at the room's law. That writer named two delivery functions with words of its own, though the law already named them, and proposed that an amendment could wait, though the law says a standing direction made permanent becomes one. Both were corrected within the day, the first as pull request #92 and the second by the amendment recorded as RoR-26. The same day that amendment's own proposal missed one reader of the clauses it changed. None of this was ignorance of where the law is kept. In each case the law was not on the screen when the work was done. The operator directed this tool the same day: what a pointer asks for, a print delivers.

The first draft of this tool repeated the fault it was built against. It chose a resident's clauses by a word in that resident's registry entry, and so assigned the work by identity, which STELA's own `open` says it does not do. The operator stopped that draft the same evening, before it landed. This version reads a function from the open packet's leaf and from nowhere else, and its table refuses any row that is not one of the four functions.

## Integrity Filter Warnings

- **Shown is not heeded.** An OPEN proves that the law was on the caller's screen. Whether the work obeys it is still for the gates and the Auditor to say.
- **It locks nothing yet.** Until a writing tool calls `standing`, an OPEN is asked for and not required. That change is its own packet, and it follows the day every resident of the team has made a first OPEN, so that the lock cannot shut out the team that must mend it.
- **A function is known only while its packet is open.** Before a packet opens nobody is named, and an OPEN made then shows all four rows. A resident whose last OPEN showed one function, and who then drafts the next packet, has not been shown the Integrator's clauses, and `--check` says so only once the leaf names it. The lock closes this: each station, the draft included, will ask for an OPEN that showed its own function.
- **A worker is its parent.** A worker carries its parent's signal, so the tool cannot tell them apart (PoT 3.5). A worker does not run OPEN; its parent's OPEN stands for the work the parent answers for.
- **A hand edit of a private file is out of reach.** The tool can gate only what goes through the room's tools. Its own ledger is such a file: a record is evidence that an OPEN was made, not proof against a hand that writes one. The trace in each report line and the digest of what was printed let an auditor compare.
- **A part can still be cut.** The part size is one constant, set under the smallest output a provider's shell tool is known to pass whole. A resident who sees a part that does not end with its own closing line stops and reports it, and the constant is lowered by a packet.
- **The table is a choice.** Which clauses every OPEN repeats is changed by a packet, like any other tracked text. The suite holds it short: the freshness lines and the clauses for every reader and for all four functions are read as one part.
- **A change of the law ends every OPEN.** After an amendment lands, each resident is shown the changed file whole at its next OPEN. An amendment that retires a cited clause changes the table in the same packet (PoT 7.2), or the suite fails.
- **Orientation is not law.** The board and the packet status are printed as they stand and grant nothing (PoT 1.3.3).
- **Tests are synthetic.** They build their own rooms and registries, and stand in for the packet tool's status and the gauge except where a case says otherwise. Whether each provider's shell passes a part whole on the host is proved only by the residents' first OPENs.
